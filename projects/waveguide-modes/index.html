    Metal Waveguide Modes in HFSS, Checked by Hand

    Coursework (instructor: Prof. Berardi Sensale-Rodriguez): HFSS Tutorial 4 and homework 4.

    01

    The guide and its modes

    A hollow metal waveguide carries a wave only above a cutoff frequency, and each field pattern, or mode, has its own cutoff. I built a rectangular copper guide in Ansys HFSS, 22.86 × 10.16 mm inside and 50 mm long, with a wave port at each end. HFSS solves each port for its modes, and I asked for the first four from 2 to 20 GHz.

    On the 1 GHz sweep the four modes start to travel between 6 and 7, 13 and 14, 14 and 15 and 16 and 17 GHz. The closed-form cutoffs are 6.56, 13.11, 14.75 and 16.15 GHz. Above cutoff the HFSS phase constant stays within about 0.1% of the closed-form value at every sweep point.

    Closed form: β = √(k² − kc²), with k = ω/c and kc² = (mπ/a)² + (nπ/b)². Below its cutoff a mode decays instead of traveling, and there HFSS matches the closed-form decay constant within 0.2%. TE11 and TM11 share one cutoff, so the fourth port mode is one of that pair.

    02

    Empty and filled

    Then I filled the same guide with a dielectric, relative permittivity 2.56 and loss tangent 0.000255, and ran the same sweep. The wave is slower in the dielectric, so every cutoff drops by a factor of √2.56 = 1.6 and the phase constant rises.

    The first cutoff moves from 6.56 to 4.10 GHz, and at 20 GHz the phase constant goes from 396 to 656 rad/m. In the empty guide only the copper walls lose power, 0.11 dB/m at 10 GHz. With the dielectric the loss is 0.53 dB/m at 10 GHz and grows to 0.90 dB/m at 20 GHz.

    The closed-form wall loss and dielectric loss of the first mode add up to the HFSS values within 0.1% in both guides. At 10 GHz in the filled guide the walls account for 0.12 dB/m and the dielectric for 0.41 dB/m. Copper conductivity 5.8 × 10⁷ S/m.

    03

    Checked by hand

    A homework problem asked for the same filled guide by hand at 10 GHz: cutoff frequency, guide wavelength, wave impedance, phase velocity and group velocity. I also modeled it in HFSS, so the two can be compared number by number.

    By hand the guide wavelength is 2.056 cm. The HFSS phase constant at 10 GHz, 305.9 rad/m, gives 2.054 cm, and in the HFSS field plot the maxima sit 10.2 mm apart, half a guide wavelength.

    The hand values use c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s and the cutoff rounded to 4.1 GHz. The homework model was swept in 0.5 GHz steps. Phase and group velocity by hand multiply to (c/1.6)². The HFSS group velocity is the slope of ω against β between 9 and 11 GHz. The wave impedance by hand is 258.4 Ω.

    04

    A circular guide

    A second homework problem was a circular copper guide of 3 cm radius. By hand I found the cutoff of its first mode, TE11, and its copper wall loss at 7 GHz. In HFSS I solved the first four port modes from 1 to 15 GHz.

    By hand the TE11 cutoff is 2.93 GHz; in HFSS the first mode is cut off at 2 GHz and travels at 3 GHz. The wall loss of TE11 at 7 GHz is 0.011 dB/m. TE01, with its cutoff at 6.1 GHz, loses 0.026 dB/m at 7 GHz, more than twice as much, because 7 GHz is just above its cutoff.

    Surface resistance of copper at 7 GHz: 0.0219 Ω (σ = 5.76 × 10⁷ S/m). HFSS returns the two polarizations of TE11 as port modes 1 and 2, which lie on one curve. Mode 3 starts between 3 and 4 GHz and mode 4 between 4 and 5 GHz, where TM01 (3.83 GHz) and TE21 (4.86 GHz) have their cutoffs. The loss values are hand calculations; the HFSS plot shows the phase constant only.

    05

    Course and scope

    Course
    Coursework for ECE 6310, Advanced Electromagnetic Fields (Prof. Berardi Sensale-Rodriguez), Spring 2026: HFSS Tutorial 4 (the rectangular guide, empty and filled) and homework 4, problems 8.2 and 9.14 of Balanis, Advanced Engineering Electromagnetics.
    My part
    I built and ran the HFSS models, did the homework calculations by hand, and compared the results. The rectangular guide follows the course tutorial.
    Tools
    Ansys HFSS 2025 R2: wave ports with four modes, discrete frequency sweeps, port propagation constants, field plots. The plots on this page were made in Python from the HFSS exports.
    Scope
    Hollow metal guides only, empty or filled with a dielectric. Dielectric guides without metal walls, such as slabs and optical fibers, are not covered here.

    The EIT-like terahertz metasurface, my project in the same course, also modeled in Ansys HFSS.
