    Photon Upconversion in CdSe–Anthracene Hybrids

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, Farwa Awan, Tsumugi Miyashita, Todd D. Krauss, and Ming Lee Tang, University of Utah & University of Rochester.
    Poster presented at the 2nd International Symposium on Photochemistry & Functional Interfaces (ISPF2), New York, 2024.
    Poster

    01

    The relay

    Two green photons become one violet photon through triplet–triplet annihilation (TTA). A CdSe nanoplatelet absorbs the 532 nm light, an anthracenecarboxylic acid (ACA) bound to its surface takes the triplet by Dexter transfer, and a free emitter in solution, 9,10-diphenylanthracene (DPA), collects it and emits at 410 nm.

    2.34 eV in at 532 nm, 3.06 eV out at 410 nm. The triplet steps down from the platelet to ACA at 1.83 eV and on to DPA at 1.77 eV; when two DPA triplets meet, TTA lifts one to the emitting singlet.

    Two Dexter steps, TET1 to the mediator and TET2 to the emitter, then TTA. In this work the Tang lab's CdSe quantum dots were replaced with nanoplatelets.

    02

    Nanoplatelets and three isomers

    CdSe nanoplatelets (NPLs) are quantum-confined only in their thickness, which is set to the atomic layer. There is no size polydispersity to broaden the spectra, the enhanced exchange interactions favor the dark (triplet-like) exciton that drives Dexter transfer, and the absorption cross-sections are large.

    5.5 monolayers of CdSe in every platelet; three anchor positions, 1ACA, 2ACA and 9ACA, on the same platelets with the same DPA emitter.

    The carboxylate anchors ACA to the platelet surface and the anthracene relays the triplet. The ring position of the carboxylic acid changes the electronic coupling between the platelet and the aromatic core. ATR-IR confirmed that all three bind through the carboxylate.

    03

    Measured yields

    For each isomer I titrated the ligand-to-nanoplatelet ratio and measured absorption, upconverted emission and upconversion quantum yield under 532 nm excitation with 3 mM DPA in toluene.

    At each isomer's optimum loading: 9ACA 0.43%, 1ACA 0.1%, 2ACA 0.03%. The same molecule, anchored through a different ring position, spans an order of magnitude in yield.

    Bars are the quantum yields at the optimum ligand-to-nanoplatelet ratio of each isomer. The absorption and emission spectra across the loadings are on the poster.

    04

    Transient absorption

    Nanosecond transient absorption shows why the yields stay below 1%. Energy does leave the platelet once 9ACA is attached, but no clear anthracene triplet absorption grows in.

    With 9ACA the nanoplatelet kinetics at 450 to 455 nm and 500 to 505 nm are slightly quenched; at 420 to 440 nm, where the anthracene triplet would absorb, nothing rises.

    Bare 5.5 ML CdSe NPLs against NPL/9ACA, normalized ΔA. Even the best isomer leaves most of the absorbed energy unused, so the next step is the first, platelet-to-molecule transfer: controlling nanoplatelet stacking and optimizing the ligand-to-nanoplatelet ratio.
