    Photon Upconversion in CdSe–Anthracene Hybrids

    CdSe nanoplatelet–anthracene hybrids that convert two green photons into one violet photon, and how the position of one carboxylic acid on the anthracene sets the upconversion yield.

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, Farwa Awan, Tsumugi Miyashita, Todd D. Krauss, and Ming Lee Tang University of Utah & University of Rochester

    Poster presented at the 2nd International Symposium on Photochemistry & Functional Interfaces (ISPF2), New York, 2024. View the poster (PDF).

    Photon upconversion

    Photon upconversion combines two low-energy photons into one higher-energy photon through triplet–triplet annihilation (TTA). A CdSe nanoplatelet absorbs the 532 nm (2.34 eV) photons; a mediator covalently bound to its surface, here anthracenecarboxylic acid (ACA), accepts the triplet by Dexter transfer (TET1); and a free emitter in solution, 9,10-diphenylanthracene (DPA), collects it (TET2). When two DPA triplets meet, TTA promotes one to the singlet state, which emits at 410 nm (3.06 eV). In this work we replaced the Tang lab’s CdSe quantum dots with nanoplatelets.

    Nanoplatelets

    CdSe nanoplatelets (NPLs) are quantum-confined only in their thickness, which is controlled to the atomic layer (5.5 monolayers). There is no size polydispersity to broaden the spectra, the enhanced exchange interactions favor the dark (triplet-like) exciton that drives Dexter transfer, and the absorption cross-sections are large.

    Three isomers

    The carboxylate of ACA anchors it to the NPL surface and the anthracene relays the triplet. The position at which the carboxylic acid attaches changes the electronic coupling between the platelet and the aromatic core. I compared the three isomers, 1ACA, 2ACA, and 9ACA, with the same 5.5 ML CdSe NPLs and the same DPA emitter:

    Measurements

    For each isomer I titrated the ligand-to-nanoplatelet ratio and measured absorption, upconverted emission, and upconversion quantum yield under 532 nm excitation with 3 mM DPA in toluene. At each isomer’s optimum loading the ranking is 9ACA (0.43%) > 1ACA (0.1%) > 2ACA (0.03%). ATR-IR confirmed that all three bind through the carboxylate.

    Conclusions

    Attachment geometry controls triplet transfer: the same molecule, anchored through a different ring position, spans an order of magnitude in upconversion yield. Even the best isomer leaves most of the absorbed energy unused, so the next step is to improve the first, platelet-to-molecule transfer step by controlling nanoplatelet stacking and optimizing the ligand-to-nanoplatelet ratio.
