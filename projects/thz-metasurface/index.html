    Optically Tunable THz Metasurface Absorber

    An optically tunable terahertz metasurface absorber on Fe-doped β-Ga₂O₃: HFSS design, measured photoconductivity, the ITO electrode process, and the first resonator array on the crystal. Poster at IRMMW-THz 2026.

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, Steve Blair, and Berardi Sensale-Rodriguez
    Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering, University of Utah

    Poster at the 51st International Conference on Infrared, Millimeter, and Terahertz Waves (IRMMW-THz 2026), Salt Lake City: Monday, October 12, 2026, 17:30 to 19:00, Poster Session 1, Board 1. Poster (PDF). An earlier version was presented at the ASCENT Summer School on Semiconductor Science & Technology, University of Michigan, August 2026 (poster).

    Abstract

    We present an optically tunable terahertz (THz) metasurface absorber based on Fe-doped β-Ga2O3, an ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor (Eg ≈ 4.8 eV). Despite this large bandgap, Fe acceptor states enable sub-bandgap photoexcitation at 808 nm, producing pump-power-dependent photoconductivity. Full-wave electromagnetic simulations for σ = 0 to 25 S/m show 82.2% absorption modulation and 95.0% reflected-power modulation at 300 GHz, establishing Fe:β-Ga2O3 as a promising platform for optically reconfigurable THz photonics.

    Device design

    A gold H-shaped resonator array on a 724 μm thick Fe:β-Ga2O3 substrate over a gold ground plane: P = 150 μm, a = 120 μm, w = 15 μm, g = 5 μm, Au 0.2 μm. The topology follows the split-ring design of [2], scaled for 300 GHz and simulated in ANSYS HFSS 2025 R2.

    Results

    In the dark state (σ = 0), the device reaches 95.8% absorption at 300.4 GHz. As σ increases toward the pump-limited ~25 S/m [1], absorption decreases to 17.0% and reflected power increases from 4.2% to 83.0%: 82.2% absorption modulation and 95.0% reflected-power modulation.

    ITO version for pumping through the pattern

    Gold blocks the pump, so this version uses ITO for both the resonators and the ground plane. On the birefringent (010) cut, four simulated reflection channels switch by more than 10 dB between dark and illuminated states (TE at 238 and 682 GHz, TM at 346 and 419 GHz) with 4.0 to 4.7 dB insertion loss.

    Measured photoconductivity

    I measured our own Fe:β-Ga₂O₃ crystals by THz transmission with the 808 nm pump off and on: the photoinduced conductivity rises superlinearly with pump power and reaches 6.8 S/m at 20 W (Fig. 2a), below the ~25 S/m of [1] used in the simulation.

    At 463 GHz, stepping the pump from 5 W to 20 W lowers the transmitted amplitude by 86% (Fig. 2b); 37 s after the pump is switched off the signal is back to 90% of its pump-off level, a recovery that most likely comes from heating of the sample.

    Measurements performed on Fe:β-Ga₂O₃ crystals from the groups of Prof. Kai Fu and Prof. Michael Scarpulla.

    First resonator array on the crystal

    Figure 3 shows the latest array: H-shaped resonators in 0.5 µm of sputtered ITO on Fe:β-Ga₂O₃, a = 99 µm, w = 15.5 µm and g = 19 µm as fabricated, and the unannealed film reads 20 Ω/□ (σ = 9.4 × 10⁴ S/m). Every cell survived lift-off.

    Its THz transmission matches HFSS to 0.04 rms from 300 to 650 GHz with nothing fitted; at 20 Ω/□ the resonance is heavily damped. Next, I will anneal the ITO toward 3.7 × 10⁵ S/m, add the ground plane and measure reflection under the pump.

    ITO electrode: 4.9 Ω/□ at 384 nm

    The HFSS thickness sweep asked for 380 nm; my sputter recipe now delivers 384 nm, and two independent runs annealed in forming gas both reach 4.9 Ω/□, down from near 19 Ω/□ as deposited and 2.4 times better than the best 150 nm film (12 Ω/□). Both thicknesses give σ ≈ 5.3 to 5.6 × 10⁵ S/m.
