    Optically Tunable THz Metasurface Absorber

    turn up the pump and the absorber becomes a mirror scroll to run the experiment ↓

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, Afrouz Taherian, and Berardi Sensale-Rodriguez
    Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering, University of Utah

    Poster at the 51st International Conference on Infrared, Millimeter, and Terahertz Waves (IRMMW-THz 2026), Salt Lake City: Monday, October 12, 2026, 17:30 to 19:00, Poster Session 1, Board 1. Poster (PDF). An earlier version was presented at the ASCENT Summer School on Semiconductor Science & Technology, University of Michigan, August 2026 (poster).

    Abstract

    We present an optically tunable terahertz (THz) metasurface absorber based on Fe-doped β-Ga2O3, an ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor (Eg ≈ 4.8 eV). Despite this large bandgap, Fe acceptor states enable sub-bandgap photoexcitation at 808 nm, producing pump-power-dependent photoconductivity. Full-wave electromagnetic simulations for σ = 0 to 25 S/m show 82.2% absorption modulation and 95.0% reflected-power modulation at 300 GHz, establishing Fe:β-Ga2O3 as a promising platform for optically reconfigurable THz photonics.

    Introduction

    β-Ga2O3 is an ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor of interest for power electronics and UV photonics. In Fe-doped β-Ga2O3, mid-gap Fe acceptor states lie approximately 0.85 eV below the conduction band. Under sub-bandgap 808 nm continuous-wave illumination, electrons are excited from these acceptors into the conduction band, producing THz photoconductivity that increases with pump power [1]. Jiang et al. reported photoinduced conductivities up to ~25 S/m at 2 W pump power [1]. This response motivates THz metasurfaces that use photoinduced conductivity for tuning without electrical contacts, gates, or temperature control.

    Device design

    The device is a gold H-shaped resonator array on a 724 μm thick Fe:β-Ga2O3 substrate backed by a continuous gold ground plane. The unit-cell parameters are P = 150 μm, a = 120 μm, w = 15 μm, g = 5 μm, and Au thickness 0.2 μm. The resonator topology follows the split-ring design of [2], scaled for 300 GHz operation on Fe:β-Ga2O3. The H shape gives a strong inductive-capacitive resonance near 300 GHz, and with the ground plane blocking transmission, the incident THz power is partitioned only between reflection and absorption. Full-wave simulations were performed in ANSYS HFSS 2025 R2 with adaptive meshing at 0.32 THz (20 passes, ΔS < 0.01) and a 280 to 320 GHz sweep over 12 conductivity values (σ = 0 to 25 S/m).

    Results

    In the dark state (σ = 0), the device reaches 95.8% absorption at 300.4 GHz, owed to ohmic losses in the finite-conductivity metals. As σ in the Fe-doped β-Ga2O3 increases toward the pump-limited value of ~25 S/m [1], the resonator becomes progressively overcoupled: absorption decreases to 17.0%, while reflected power increases from 4.2% to 83.0%. The corresponding absorption modulation is 82.2%; the reflected-power modulation, defined between the dark and illuminated states, is 95.0%. Since the ground plane suppresses transmission for all σ, the device operates as an optically controlled reflection-mode THz modulator.

    Across the 12 conductivity states the resonance at 300.4 GHz does not shift as σ increases; it flattens out. Most of the change happens between 0.5 and 10 S/m, inside the range an 808 nm pump can reach.

    An ITO version that can be pumped through the pattern

    Gold blocks the pump, so the device that can be illuminated through its own pattern uses ITO for both the resonators and the ground plane. On the (010) cut, β-Ga2O3 is birefringent, so the TE and TM resonances sit at different frequencies. In simulation, four reflection channels switch by more than 10 dB between the dark and illuminated states (TE at 238 and 682 GHz, TM at 346 and 419 GHz) with 4.0 to 4.7 dB insertion loss.

    Choosing the ITO thickness

    An HFSS sweep of ITO thickness from 100 to 500 nm answers how thick the electrode should be. Both polarizations clear the 10 dB extinction target over most of the range, though neither curve is monotonic: TM peaks near 300 nm and TE near 400 nm. 500 nm gives the best combination, 16 to 17 dB extinction for TE and TM at the lowest insertion loss (3.9 to 4.2 dB), with 380 nm as a fabrication-friendly backup.

    Measured photoconductivity of the substrate

    The simulation takes σ = 0 to 25 S/m from a published measurement [1]. Before building on that number, I measured the photoconductivity of our own Fe:β-Ga₂O₃ crystals under the 808 nm pump, at terahertz frequencies.

    The measurement is a transmission experiment on a bare crystal with the CW photomixing spectrometer: THz signal through the crystal with the pump off, again with the pump on, and the ratio converted to a conductivity with the thin-film relation σ = (1 + ns)/(Z₀·d) · (1/T − 1), with d the plate thickness. The photoinduced conductivity rises superlinearly with pump power and reaches 6.8 S/m at 20 W (Fig. 2a). A second setup, a VDI source at 300 GHz, gives the same trend; its absolute values differ by up to a factor of four depending on the detection channel, so I quote a range rather than a settled number until the two setups are reconciled.

    Figure 2b shows the same effect as a switch. At 463 GHz, stepping the pump from 5 W to 20 W lowers the transmitted amplitude by 86%. After the pump is switched off, the signal is back to 90% of its pump-off level 37 s later. Photoexcitation from the Fe level is sub-picosecond, so a recovery over tens of seconds most likely comes from heating of the sample; we are looking into the temperature dependence.

    These conductivities sit below the ~25 S/m that Jiang et al. report for nominally the same material [1]. Beam size does not explain the gap: 20 W on our 10 mm spot is about 25 W/cm², above their 2 W on a 5 mm spot (about 10 W/cm²). The crystals differ in Fe and donor content, and so does the extraction: we apply the thin-film relation across the whole plate, and their value is read from a figure.

    The first resonator array on the crystal

    The ITO device is now in fabrication. Figure 3 shows the latest array: H-shaped resonators in 0.5 µm of sputtered ITO on Fe:β-Ga₂O₃, written with the DWL 66+ laser writer and lifted off from an LOR 10B / S1813 bilayer (AZ 300 MIF develop to a 2.5 µm undercut, O₂ descum, acetone and MIF lift-off). As fabricated, a = 99 µm, w = 15.5 µm and g = 19 µm, and the film reads 20 Ω/□ by four-point probe (σ = 9.4 × 10⁴ S/m). Every cell survived lift-off. There is no ground plane yet and the ITO is not annealed.

    Its THz transmission, measured against a same-day air reference, matches HFSS to 0.04 rms from 300 to 650 GHz. The model uses the as-fabricated dimensions, the film’s DC conductivity and the permittivity of a bare spot on the same piece; nothing was fitted on the array. At 20 Ω/□ the resonance is heavily damped, so the array mainly lowers the transmission, and HFSS predicts the same broad response. The next steps are to anneal the ITO toward 3.7 × 10⁵ S/m, add the ground plane, and measure reflection under the pump.

    Earlier arrays on Ga₂O₃ came out of lift-off incomplete. The cause was the LOR develop step: the undercut was 0.7 to 0.9 µm on 1.2 µm of LOR, so the sputtered ITO stayed continuous over the resist step and tore during lift-off. Developing to a 2.5 to 2.7 µm undercut and adding an O₂ descum before deposition fixed it.

    Transparent conductors at terahertz frequencies

    The ITO device needs an electrode that passes the 808 nm pump and still conducts at 300 GHz. I sputter ITO films on Ga2O3, sapphire and glass (Denton Discovery 18), extract their sheet resistance at terahertz frequencies from CW photomixing transmission, and compare it with DC four-point probe, because a film that conducts well at DC can behave differently at 0.3 THz.

    Comparing the same sapphire piece before and after about 160 nm of ITO, the Fabry–Pérot fringes carry two pieces of information. The fringe spacing (~114 GHz) gives the substrate thickness, about 0.43 mm, and the fringe amplitude gives the film’s conductivity: peak transmission drops from a mean of 0.95 (bare) to 0.44 (coated).

    Extracting sheet resistance from the THz data with the Tinkham thin-film formula and comparing it with the four-point probe shows the gap between DC and THz behavior in the disordered, not yet annealed film: the THz value reads roughly twice the DC one.

    4.9 Ω/□ at 384 nm

    The thickness sweep asked for 380 nm, so I adjusted the sputter recipe until the tool delivered that thickness on demand: 384 nm by profilometry, about 1% above target (Denton Discovery 18: 50 W DC, Ar/O₂ 28.3/10.3 sccm, 3.86 mTorr, 34 min 30 s). Two independent runs at that thickness came out of the chamber near 19 Ω/□, and a forming-gas anneal brought both to 4.9 Ω/□, reproduced across three probe points per sample with under 3% spread and 2.4 times better than the best 150 nm film (12 Ω/□). Both thicknesses give σ ≈ 5.3 to 5.6 × 10⁵ S/m, so the film quality holds as the layer thickens.

    One negative result from the same campaign: sputtering onto a 200 °C heated stage made everything worse. The plasma would not strike at the standard flows, the as-deposited film read about 58 Ω/□, three times the room-temperature value, and the sample burned in the anneal. Room-temperature deposition followed by the anneal is the process of record.

    The lithography is in-house as well. After qualifying on the DWL 66+ maskless writer, I drew the resonator layout, wrote and developed it, and patterned the first array in a 400 nm ITO film in August 2026. The array on Ga₂O₃ shown above came out of the same process after the lift-off fix.

    Measured THz anisotropy of the substrate

    β-Ga₂O₃ is monoclinic, and CW-THz transmission through two crystals at 0° and 90° polarization shows the Fabry–Pérot fringes shifting and re-spacing between the two orientations. That is a direct measure of the birefringence in optical thickness, about 9 to 12%.
