    How I Made the THz Resonator Array

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, University of Utah Nanofab, September to October 2026.
    Part of the optically tunable THz metasurface project. Every photo on this page is from my own runs.

    01

    Writing the pattern

    The resonators are written directly on the crystal with the Heidelberg DWL 66+ maskless laser writer, so a new design needs no new photomask. To pick the exposure I compared laser powers on the same cell.

    H-shaped cells at 150 µm pitch; exposure at 220 mW.

    The comparison shows the same developed cell at 220 and 230 mW, measured on the microscope.

    02

    The bilayer resist

    The pattern is lifted off from two resist layers: LOR 10B underneath and S1813 on top. The developer eats the LOR sideways under the S1813 edge. That undercut breaks the sputtered ITO at every edge, so the resist and the ITO on it lift off cleanly and only the resonators stay on the crystal.

    Developed cell: 102 µm outer edge, 17 µm center gap.

    LOR is not photosensitive; only the developer removes it, so the develop time sets the undercut.

    03

    The first lift-off

    On the first Ga₂O₃ piece the lift-off did not finish. Many cells kept an ITO "lid" over the opening, and under the Keyence some frames came out ragged or with pieces missing.

    About 10 to 20% of the cells defective.

    The piece was still good enough to measure at THz in the following days. Longer sonication removed the lids but started to lift the pattern too, so more time in the solvent was not the answer.

    04

    The undercut

    The cause was the develop step. After 8 s the undercut was under 1 µm on about 1.2 µm of LOR, so the openings never cleared: the ITO landed on resist left in the openings and tore when that resist came off.

    0.79 µm undercut after 8 s; 2.56 to 2.76 µm after 16 s.

    On glass in July the undercut had been too large (7.6 µm), and the first Ga₂O₃ run overcorrected. A later piece with a 1.1 µm undercut lost every cell in the final strip. The rule that worked: an undercut of 2 to 3 µm, checked on the microscope before deposition, and a short descum after develop.

    05

    Every cell intact

    On the final device every cell came through lift-off, with no lids left. Drag the divider to compare the first device with this one at the same scale.

    2.2 to 2.6 µm undercut after a 25 s develop; about 0.5 µm of sputtered ITO.

    This piece was polished (100 to 150 nm roughness on the front). It is not perfect: thin ITO strips fell onto a few cells, and every edge has a narrow colored band where the ITO thins under the resist overhang.

    The same field of view on both devices, about 5 by 4 cells at 150 µm pitch (100 µm scale bar on the right image).

    06

    Measured against the model

    The finished array was measured on the CW THz spectrometer and compared with HFSS using the measured film conductivity and the dimensions from the microscope, with nothing fitted on the array.

    Within 0.04 rms of HFSS over 300 to 650 GHz.

    Measured as deposited, before annealing and without the ground plane.
