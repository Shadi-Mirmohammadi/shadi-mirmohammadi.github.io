    Excitation-Switchable Eu³⁺ Luminescence in GdScO₃

    A nominally undoped crystal changes its emission color with the excitation wavelength, and its luminescence reports on substrate quality.

    Seyedeh Shadi Mirmohammadi, Afrouz Taherian, Yunshan Wang, Diego P. Fernandez, Michael A. Scarpulla, Steve Blair, and Berardi Sensale-Rodriguez University of Utah

    Manuscript in preparation for Optical Materials Express (2026).

    Visible emission from a nominally undoped substrate

    GdScO₃ is a perovskite substrate for epitaxial oxide films. We studied commercial, nominally undoped single crystals from two vendors (Stonylab (100) and CrysTec (110)); under deep-UV excitation they show bright, sharp visible emission. The emission comes from europium at the parts-per-million level, an unintentional trace impurity (1–2 ppm, roughly one Eu³⁺ ion per 10⁵–10⁶ Gd³⁺). The trace emits strongly because energy absorbed anywhere in the Gd³⁺ sublattice migrates to the Eu³⁺ sites.

    Emission color versus excitation wavelength

    With the tunable deep-UV source (third-harmonic generation of a Ti:sapphire laser) I built for my GeO₂ photodetector measurements, we swept the excitation from 236 to 270 nm. At short excitation wavelengths the emission is dominated by the Eu³⁺ ⁵D₂/⁵D₁ lines (491 nm and the 535–555 nm group). With the excitation tuned into the Gd³⁺ ⁶IJ absorption near 266 nm, the emission is almost pure red ⁵D₀ → ⁷F₁,₂ at 590 and 612 nm.

    The intensity ratio I₄₉₁/I₆₁₂ falls by more than an order of magnitude across the sweep, crossing unity near 251 nm, and its descending branch follows a single exponential (k = 0.124 nm⁻¹). In CIE color coordinates the emission moves continuously from yellow-green to orange.

    Identifying the emitter

    Sharp lines at 491, 590, and 612 nm could come from several rare-earth ions, so we measured the trace impurities in both crystals by LA-ICP-MS. The crystals have nearly disjoint impurity fingerprints but the same sharp-line emission at identical wavelengths, so the emitter must be an impurity they share. Only Dy and Eu are visible-active in both, and Dy³⁺ would emit at 482 and 575 nm in this host, which we never see. The line positions match Eu-doped GdScO₃ from the literature, and the excitation behavior matches Gd³⁺ → Eu³⁺ sensitization.

    Screening substrates with light

    The two crystals differ most in a broad defect-emission band, strong in one, nearly absent in the other, and uncorrelated with the measured impurity content. Annealing one crystal at 1200 °C in air suppresses this band almost completely while the sharp Eu³⁺ lines survive, and raises the Eu³⁺ ⁷F₂/⁷F₁ asymmetry ratio from 1.30 to 1.90. UV illumination of a wafer reports both its energy-transfer pathways and its defect signatures, a fast, nondestructive optical screen for scandate substrates.

    X-ray rocking curves confirm the anneal structurally: the FWHM narrows from 0.063° to 0.024°, and the peak is 2.6× narrower and 2.8× brighter.
