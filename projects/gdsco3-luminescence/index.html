    Excitation-Selective Trace Rare-Earth Luminescence in GdScO3

    01

    Trace emitters in an undoped substrate

    GdScO₃ is a perovskite substrate for epitaxial oxide films and is usually treated as optically inert. Two commercial, nominally undoped crystals, Stonylab (100) and CrysTec (110), show sharp visible lines under deep-UV excitation. LA-ICP-MS traces them to europium and terbium at the parts-per-million level.

    Eu at 1–2 ppm in both crystals and Tb at about 1 ppm in one of them: roughly one emitting ion per 10⁵–10⁶ Gd³⁺, thousands of times below intentional doping.

    The two crystals carry different impurity fingerprints (Th and U appear only in the CrysTec crystal) but the same Eu³⁺ lines at identical wavelengths. Concentrations are semiquantitative (Gd internal standard against NIST 610 glass).

    02

    The excitation switch

    Tuning the excitation from 236 to 270 nm changes which lines dominate. Below about 255 nm the spectrum carries lines at 437, 491 and 543 nm next to the red Eu³⁺ ⁵D₀ → ⁷F₁,₂ lines at 590 and 612 nm; by 263 nm only the red lines remain.

    I₄₉₁/I₆₁₂ falls from 1.03 to 0.13 between 250 and 263 nm excitation and by more than an order of magnitude across the series; its descending branch follows a single exponential with k = 0.110 ± 0.006 nm⁻¹. In CIE coordinates the emission moves from pale blue to orange.

    Both quantities come from single spectra, so no power correction is needed; single-spectrum ratios scatter by ±23%. The same switch appears in three crystals measured in one session. The animation blends the two measured end spectra of Crystal A (236 and 266.6 nm, each normalized to its own maximum); the frames between them are a linear blend, not measurements.

    04

    Which ion switches

    The 491 nm line can come from Eu³⁺ (⁵D₂ → ⁷F₂) or from Tb³⁺ (⁵D₄ → ⁷F₆). Following each line across the excitation series separates the two: the 437, 491 and 543 nm lines fall together as the excitation passes 255 nm, the 437 nm line has no Eu³⁺ counterpart, and the other Eu³⁺ ⁵D₂ lines at 465 and 514 nm do not fall.

    The switching lines are consistent with Tb³⁺, whose excitation band in GdScO₃ ends near 255 nm, but they are not uniquely assigned. Excitation on the Gd³⁺ ⁶IJ lines (276–288 nm) gives no detectable Eu³⁺ emission, so these Gd levels do not feed Eu³⁺ at this concentration.

    Level energies from Carnall et al. (ions in LaF₃). The absorber that excites the red Eu³⁺ lines at 263–267 nm is not identified by the transmission data.

    05

    Annealing and XRD

    The crystals differ most in a broad band from 420 to 660 nm, strong in the CrysTec crystal and almost absent in the Stonylab one. One half of a second CrysTec substrate was annealed for 60 h at 1200 °C in air; PL was compared before and after the anneal, and XRD on both halves.

    Relative to the sharp Eu³⁺ lines the band drops about fivefold under 250 nm and sevenfold under 266.6 nm excitation, while the Eu³⁺ ⁷F₂/⁷F₁ ratio changes by only 3–9%, within the spread from remounting. XRD gives d₁₁₀ = 3.968 Å for both halves; the annealed half has a narrower 220 rocking curve, 0.026° against 0.067°.

    The band matches none of the detected trivalent impurities and is consistent with a defect-related origin; damage under the lapped back face and divalent rare-earth ions remain possible sources. The spectra before and after annealing were recorded on different spectrometer units, so each is normalized to the 590 nm line.
