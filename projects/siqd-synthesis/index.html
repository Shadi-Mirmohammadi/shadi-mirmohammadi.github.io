    Silicon Quantum Dots from Plasma to Surface Chemistry

    Shadi Mirmohammadi, Ming Lee Tang group, Department of Chemistry, University of Utah. M.S. research, 2024–2025; final oral exam, April 28, 2025.

    01

    Plasma synthesis

    I grew the silicon quantum dots in the lab's non-thermal plasma reactor. Silane diluted in argon flows with hydrogen through a quartz tube under vacuum. A radio-frequency discharge between two grounded electrodes nucleates silicon nanocrystals in flight, and hydrogen bubbled through 1-dodecene caps them in the afterglow, so they come out soluble in toluene.

    Batches from February and June 2025: 2.9 nm dots with photoluminescence at 720 nm, and a larger 3.4 nm batch at 780 nm.

    Each run starts with the vacuum: pump down, zero the gauge at base pressure, and time the leak rate, the time the closed system takes to rise by 0.01 Torr, before any gas flows. The 1-dodecene in the bubbler is degassed by freeze–pump–thaw in liquid nitrogen. The plasma is ignited and matched in argon, then switched to the silane mixture, with the pressure held by a throttle valve under PID control; higher pressure gives larger dots. At the end the reactor is back-filled with argon to glovebox pressure and the collection chamber goes into the glovebox.

    02

    Grafting the ligands

    The dodecane-capped dots still carry Si–H bonds on the surface. After a 1.5 h anneal at 180 °C in mesitylene to reduce surface defects, I attached four molecules by radical hydrosilylation with AIBN as the initiator, at 65 °C for at least 15 hours.

    Four conjugates: two anthracenes joined to the surface by a saturated ethyl link (Si–9EA) or a conjugated vinyl link (Si–9VA), 9,10-bis(phenylethynyl)anthracene (Si–BPEA) and a naphthalene diimide (Si–NDI).

    Each product was washed three times: precipitated with methanol, centrifuged at 14,200 rpm for 20 min and redispersed in toluene. The ligands bind through terminal alkene or alkyne groups, which form Si–C bonds.

    03

    Counting ligands per dot

    Each ligand adds its own absorption bands between 300 and 500 nm. I scaled the spectrum of the dodecane-capped dots to each sample between 550 and 700 nm, where no ligand absorbs, subtracted it, and converted what remained to a ligand concentration. The dot concentration comes from the Si QD absorption at 488 nm.

    First batch, February 2025: 5.53 ligands per dot for 9EA, 3.59 for 9VA, 3.82 for BPEA and 2.56 for NDI.

    Extinction coefficients: Si QD 10,000 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ at 488 nm; 9EA referenced to 9-methylanthracene, 8,413 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ at 389 nm, with a 6 nm red shift for the bound molecule; BPEA 60,900 M⁻¹cm⁻¹ at 470 nm.

    04

    More ligands, two sizes

    For stronger EPR signals, the June 2025 conjugates were made at higher concentration, 1.125 mM against 0.3 mM, and with more ligand. BPEA went onto both dot sizes.

    On the 2.9 nm dots the loading rose to 6.97 for 9EA, 7.11 for 9VA and 7.22 for BPEA; on the 3.4 nm dots BPEA reached 5.35.

    With BPEA attached, the Si QD emission at 720 or 780 nm nearly disappears. Spectra are normalized to the absorption at 488 nm, excited at 488 nm, in toluene at room temperature.

    05

    What the spins showed

    The conjugates were measured by continuous-wave and time-resolved EPR at Argonne: X-band (9.7 GHz), 20 K, frozen toluene, 480 nm excitation. The three anthracene conjugates form a spin-polarized triplet on the ligand; Si–NDI forms a spin-correlated radical pair instead.

    CW EPR: the bare dots and the anthracene conjugates show one narrow line at g ≈ 1.998, about 0.6 mT wide; Si–NDI adds a line at g ≈ 2.004. The free ligands gave no signal, and no sample gave a signal before illumination.

    The anthracene triplet results are published in Nano Letters (2026); see the spin-state write-up. Next steps from the exam: higher ligand loading, more dot sizes, transient absorption, and measuring the band edges of the dots.
