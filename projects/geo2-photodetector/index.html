    01

    Solar-blind detectors

    Germanium dioxide is an emerging ultrawide-bandgap semiconductor, and a detector built on it responds only in the solar-blind ultraviolet, where sunlight does not reach the ground. Dr. Kai Fu's group fabricated metal–semiconductor–metal detectors on their GeO2 films, and the quantitative test is how the responsivity varies with wavelength across the band edge.

    Undoped rutile r-GeO2(111) grown by MOCVD on r-TiO2(111); interdigitated Ni/Au contacts (100 nm) with 20 µm finger spacing and a 250 × 300 µm active area; tunable light from 233 to 267 nm by third-harmonic generation of a Ti:sapphire laser.

    The source is my UV-pumped photoluminescence setup in Prof. Blair's lab, realigned for this experiment; I calibrated the power at each wavelength on a Thorlabs power meter with a UV extension. Responsivity is (Iphoto − Idark) / Pincident from dual-sweep I–V curves between −10 and +10 V in the dark and under illumination. The beam is ~7 mm across and the device 250 × 300 µm, so the incident power is corrected by the beam-to-device area ratio of 512.87.

    02

    Measured responsivity

    The response was measured wavelength by wavelength across the band edge from the dark and illuminated I–V sweeps. It rises toward the deepest wavelength, and with bias at every wavelength, because a stronger lateral field carries more electron–hole pairs to the fingers before they recombine.

    2.1 mA/W at 233 nm under 10 V bias, area-corrected, an external quantum efficiency of 1.12%.

    The plotted points are the uncorrected values per total beam power at 240 to 256.67 nm: the 10 V points from the dual I–V sweeps, the 4 and 8 V points from the Fu-group responsivity table. The 233 nm headline lies outside the plotted range and carries the 512.87 area correction.

    03

    Dark current and contrast

    In the dark the detector passes only picoamperes, so the light-to-dark contrast is large. The dark current is lowest not at 0 V but at a bias that moves with the sweep direction.

    Dark current in the 10⁻¹² A range from −10 to +10 V; photo-to-dark current ratio up to about 4 × 10³.

    The offset points to two slightly different metal/GeO2 contacts, which leave a small built-in field at zero bias. Charge trapped near the surface during a sweep adds to it, so the minimum depends on the sweep history. The same trapped charge gives the slow tail in the switching data below.

    04

    Switching

    With the light switched on and off every 30 s, the current steps up and falls back cycle after cycle at all four wavelengths.

    10 V across the 20 µm gap, an average field of about 5 × 10³ V/cm; transients at 240, 250, 260 and 267 nm.

    The rise is faster than the measurement resolves. After light-off the current decays with a slow tail as trapped carriers are released, and the recovery is slower at 260 and 267 nm, where more of the absorption likely goes through defect or band-tail states.
